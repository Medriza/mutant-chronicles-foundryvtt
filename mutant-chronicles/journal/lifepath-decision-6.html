Decision Six: Primary Career

Which career does the character pursue? At minimum, you will go through two Primary Career phases or one Primary Career and one Iconic Career, without cost. You can stay in and repeat a career or select a new career as a third or fourth phase. Pay one Life point for Column A or B careers, or two Life points for Column C or D. All Primary Careers are non-faction specific and open to everyone.

Step One

For each Primary Career phase, roll 1d6 and consult Column A of the Primary Career Table below.

  • Free: Roll Column A, or choose the free career option provided by your education (first and second Primary Career phases only).
  • 1 Life Point: Pick from Column A or B, or roll on Column C or D.
  • 2 Life Points: Choose from Column C or D.
  • Unemployed: You may choose to be Unemployed for up to two Primary Careers and regain one Life point each time. If you choose unemployment as a third or fourth career, it does not regain a Life point.

Step Two

Raise your Earnings Rating to match your Primary Career's rating if your current rating is less.

Step Three

Mark your age as sixteen. This increases by 1d6+1 years for each Primary or Iconic Career phase, in addition to any changes from career events or career extensions. You may pay a Life point to choose the age increase of a Primary or Iconic Career phase before or after the roll.

Step Four

Roll on the Career Events Table (see next page) for each 1d6+1 roll of years spent in that career phase.

Step Five

Optional Rule: Players can arrange their life history after their build, choosing which career and events came first, second, and so on.

Extending Careers

With GM approval, you may voluntarily spend 1d6+1 more years in any career, rolling again for a career event for each extension. You do not receive any other benefits for extending the career. This does not count as an additional career, nor does it limit the number of careers you may have — it is simply a way to represent older, more experienced characters who have seen more of what life has to throw at them.

Career Effects

The bonuses for each career decision are as follows. See the Career Reference page for the full entry for your career.

  • Attribute Improvements: Add these to your Attributes if this is your first career only. On second or subsequent Primary Career phases you do NOT gain the Attribute bonuses.
  • Mandatory Skills: You receive one rank of training in all of these skills. If this is the first rank, gain Expertise 1. If you already have Expertise 1, you may use this rank to gain either Expertise or Focus ranks.
  • Elective Skills: Pick two of these skills; you gain one rank of training in each of them.
  • Signature Skill: Pick a single skill from the list that you already have at least Expertise 1 in. This becomes a Signature Skill for you, and you gain one further rank of training in it. You may not select a skill that is already a Signature Skill. A character may only have three Signature Skills — one from faction, and two from careers — so this benefit is not granted for a third or fourth career.
  • Talents: Select one of the eligible skills and one talent from the associated tree, for which you fulfil the prerequisites.
  • Earnings Rating: This is the minimum Earnings Rating for this career. If your current rating is less, increase it to this level. If equal to or higher, no change.
  • Equipment: You may take these items of equipment or assets.

Fired

If you are fired (a Career Events result), you may retain all the benefits of that career but may not repeat or extend that Primary or Iconic Career unless you spend two Life points (in addition to any other costs) and lose one Earnings Rating. If this is not your fourth Primary Career, you may enter another career next.

Consider what might have caused the firing in the context of your character. A Mishiman from a rich family may have suffered some major dishonour. A movie star might have had a failed release. A Brotherhood character might have been implicated in a Heretic plot — perhaps making it his goal to prove his innocence.
