Decision Three: Status

What social class has your character grown up in? How does this affect his earnings? This table allows you to determine what social status your character was born into, what bonuses this gives to your Attributes, what additional skill you gain, and your initial Earnings Rating.

Step One

Roll 2d6 and consult the Social Status Table below. A character may pay one Life point to select any result up to and including Upper Status. A character may pay two Life points to select the Elite Status result.

After social status has been determined, make the following adjustments: increase the two listed Attributes by one each, gain one rank of training in the listed skill, and note the Earnings Rating for that social status. This Earnings Rating is the starting value for the character and cannot be reduced below this level during character creation, though some careers may increase it.

Assets & Earnings

The different resources a character can call upon to achieve financial or reputation-based tasks are collectively referred to as that character's assets. Money, cash-in-hand, savings, jewellery, deeds, and inheritances are easy assets to define. Other assets are more conceptual, such as favours, secret contacts, blackmail, family bonds, or goods and services a character can liquidate for capital.

Assets for a starting character are equal to his Personality attribute — these are added at the end of a Lifepath when you know the character's final Personality score.

Earnings represent a character's income — a recurring or reliable resource stream such as a salary, regular stock dividends, or freelancing contracts. Elite Status starts with an Earnings Rating of 5; there is no cap, and characters are free to reach for the stars in the corporate economy of Mutant Chronicles.

Recovery of money, goods, favours, or other items of value will often add to a character's existing assets, whilst purchasing expensive equipment or vehicles may reduce them. Note that debts incurred during character creation may not be negated with assets received during character creation — they must be role-played and dealt with through gameplay. See Chapter 23: Assets, Resources & Acquisitions for more details.
